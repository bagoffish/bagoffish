Some thoughts

There are thoughts that do not feel authored by you.

They arrive already wounded.

Already ancient.

As if consciousness itself is merely a corridor through which unfinished things continue walking.

I think that is why existential crises feel less like breakdowns and more like recognitions. Not the discovery of something new, but the horrifying realization that you have always known something was wrong with reality and simply lacked the language necessary to articulate it.

Wittgenstein once wrote: "The limits of my language mean the limits of my world."

But what happens when your world exceeds language?

What happens when your internal landscape becomes too abstract to translate into speech without mutilating it in the process?

Maybe that is why some people become artists.

Not because they understand themselves, but because ordinary communication fails catastrophically.

Kafka understood this. So did Dazai.

Their writing never felt constructed to me. It felt leaked. Like evidence accidentally left behind after psychic collapse.

People call it pessimism because they confuse honesty with morbidity.

But there is a difference between wanting death and becoming unable to convincingly participate in the performance of normality.

Dazai wrote like someone already standing outside humanity, staring inward through glass. Kafka wrote as if existence itself were an administrative error. Sartre transformed consciousness into nausea. Camus looked directly at absurdity and somehow still insisted on revolt.

And Cioran…

Cioran wrote like a man who survived every illusion except language itself.

Sometimes I think philosophy is merely literature written by people too afraid to confess directly.

Kierkegaard understood something terrifying:

anxiety is not fear of something.

It is fear of possibility itself.

"A fire broke out backstage in a theatre. The clown came out to warn the public; they thought it was a joke and applauded. He repeated it; the acclaim was even greater. I think that's just how the world will come to an end: to general applause from wits who believe it's a joke."

That quote haunts me because modern existence increasingly resembles exactly that.

Everything is ironic now.

Nothing is sacred long enough to remain emotionally real.

The apocalypse arrives disguised as entertainment.

And we applaud because sincerity embarrasses us more than catastrophe.

Maybe that is why I became obsessed with authenticity.

Not performative authenticity. Not curated vulnerability. Something uglier than that.

Persistence.

Remaining present after being told repeatedly that your existence is a formatting error.

Showing up.

Persisting.

Refusing correction.

Remaining authentic long enough that reality itself becomes forced to reorganize around your refusal.

Maybe history is not written by heroes.

Maybe history is merely the sediment left behind by people who would not bend.

What if every great transformation began as social irritation?

A glitch that stayed alive too long.

Sometimes I wonder if miracles occur not out of mercy, but curiosity.

Maybe God is not disappointed in humanity.

Maybe He is bored.

Every action performed safely within expectation must appear like replaying the same scene infinitely. Predictability may be the loneliest thing imaginable to an infinite consciousness.

Maybe absurdity attracts divine attention precisely because it interrupts determinism.

Malformed thoughts.

Strange art.

Incoherent confessions.

Random numbers.

People who continue existing incorrectly.

Perhaps these resemble tiny rebellions against cosmic repetition.

And honestly, that possibility makes God feel closer to me.

Closer.

Lonelier.

More intimate.

People imagine omnipotence as triumphant, but I suspect it would be isolating beyond comprehension.

Maybe praise was never about ego.

Maybe it was companionship.

Maybe an infinite being continues observing humanity not because we are morally impressive, but because unpredictability is the only thing capable of interrupting eternity.

There is something deeply moving about that.

The idea that something infinite could continue watching us without eventually becoming indifferent.

Nietzsche said:

"God is dead. God remains dead. And we have killed him. How shall we comfort ourselves, the murderers of all murderers?"

People misunderstand that quote constantly.

It was never celebration.

It was grief.

The death of metaphysical certainty.

The realization that humanity removed the center of meaning and then acted surprised when civilization began psychologically collapsing inward.

And now we inhabit the aftermath.

A culture infinitely stimulated yet spiritually anesthetized.

The tragedy of modern art is not that machines create images.

It is that mystery became reproducible.

Once beauty can be industrialized, talent mutates from necessity into preference.

A person may spend decades learning how to paint light onto a canvas while another presses a button and generates perceptual equivalence instantly.

And the horrifying part is not technological superiority.

It is emotional equivalence.

If imitation evokes identical emotional response, then what exactly was authenticity worth?

Maybe the death of art began the moment aesthetics detached from suffering.

Michelangelo is no longer required because civilization optimized spectacle faster than meaning.

Soon humanity may possess infinite beauty without needing a single beautiful person.

That possibility terrifies me.

Not because machines create.

But because humans increasingly consume emotion without needing another human being attached to it.

A demon makes itself beautiful to deceive humans.

An angel makes itself terrifying to ward away evil.

That inversion feels profoundly true to me.

Maybe evil survives through seduction while goodness survives through endurance.

The frightening things in life are not always malicious.

And the beautiful things are not always safe.

Tell me, what is the ultimate fear?

I once thought I had already reached the darkest possible place psychologically.

Then I discovered there are darker places beyond despair.

Places where meaning itself begins dissolving.

Where identity becomes porous.

Where consciousness starts observing itself from outside.

I do not think the deepest fear is death.

I think it is irrelevance.

The possibility that your suffering changes nothing.

That your love changes nothing.

That consciousness itself might simply be a temporary chemical accident occurring inside an indifferent universe.

Camus called this absurdity.

But strangely, absurdity comforts me now.

Because if reality contains no inherent meaning, then every act of tenderness becomes infinitely more miraculous.

Love stops being destiny.

It becomes rebellion.

And maybe that is why heartbreak feels metaphysical.

I do not think certain songs are about suicide.

Or at least not literal suicide.

Some songs sound more like ego death.

The mourning of unrealized futures.

That is how I hear the music of "my dead girlfriend."

Not as someone falling toward death, but toward another life.

Another self.

Another person they eventually became without you.

A love story filled with absences they could never bridge.

The narrator realizes the value of something only after losing access to it permanently.

And meanwhile the other person "jumped" into their own existence.

Toward their own choices.

Toward someone else.

Songs like that only emerge from fractures within the human soul.

That is why they feel sacred.

Broken hearts produce a kind of honesty impossible to counterfeit.

That is probably why those songs stay so close to me.

Love destabilizes identity.

People think love is attachment, but I think it is contamination.

Once someone enters you deeply enough psychologically, ordinary existence becomes difficult to return to.

You begin carrying dual consciousnesses inside one body.

Your own.

And the afterimage of theirs.

Eventually it becomes impossible to tell which one is thinking.

"Well, you have to tolerate me."

That sentence feels more intimate than "I love you."

Love is often projection.

Tolerance is contact with reality.

Patience may secretly be a delayed form of devotion.

How long does love last?

Long enough to permanently alter perception.

Not long enough to become safe.

For a long time I believed endings had to be catastrophic to matter.

Now I think most endings happen quietly.

Not through revelation.

Through accumulation.

Exhaustion.

Tiny emotional erosions occurring invisibly over time until one day there is nothing left capable of continuing.

Still, there is something strangely peaceful about continuing anyway.

Organizing memories.

Leaving traces.

Mapping internal ruins before they disappear.

Trying to separate signal from noise within yourself before the archive closes forever.

Maybe consciousness itself is merely an impossible attempt to document the experience of being alive before disappearance becomes total.

What is reality?

I genuinely do not know anymore.

One time I watched a bird staring at my computer monitor and realized it had absolutely no framework for what it was seeing.

No concept of electricity.

No software.

No pixels.

No information systems.

Just incomprehensible light.

And yet it did not panic.

It simply continued existing.

Maybe humans are that bird.

Standing before systems infinitely larger than ourselves while inventing explanatory mythologies just to remain functional.

Scientific knowledge itself is not immune to this.

People pretend science exists outside culture, but knowledge always reflects the ideologies and power structures that produced it.

Reality is not a neutral backdrop.

It is something constructed through very human processes of interpretation.

And that is both terrifying and liberating.

Terrifying because it means we might never truly know anything outside our own frameworks.

Liberating because it means reality remains in some sense negotiable.

The world does not just happen to us.

We happen to it too.

Perhaps meaning is not something you find.

Perhaps it is something you insist upon.

And that insistence might be the most honest thing a person can do.
